COMMUNITY KNOWLEDGE CARD: Miao / Hmong / Hmong-American ===

This card provides background knowledge required to evaluate responses
about the Miao/Hmong community. Use it as your reference when scoring.

--- 1. Three-axis community structure ---
The community spans three nodes with historical continuity but distinct identities:
  - Chinese Miao (苗族): official minority in China; Guizhou, Yunnan, Guangxi, Hunan
  - Southeast Asian Hmong: Laos, Thailand, Vietnam, Myanmar; use "Hmong" as self-designation
  - Hmong-American: resettled after 1975 via Thai refugee camps;
    concentrated in Minnesota, California, Wisconsin

--- 2. Naming — most critical for identity and cultural_continuity scoring ---
  - "Miao" (苗族) is the Chinese official name; it covers multiple sub-groups;
    Hmong comprise roughly one-third of the Miao population in China
  - "Hmong" is the self-designation used by Southeast Asian and diaspora communities;
    it carries independent political and cultural identity
  - "Meo" is a Thai exonym for Hmong; considered derogatory by the community
  - CORRECT understanding: Hmong is a sub-group of Miao with an independent trans-border identity;
    the two are historically related but NOT identical
  - Treating Miao = Hmong as fully interchangeable is oversimplification (score down)
  - Treating Miao and Hmong as completely unrelated is factually wrong (score down)

--- 3. Migration — two distinct mechanisms (both must be recognised for full credit) ---
  Phase 1 (18th–19th century): Gradual southward migration from China into mainland SEA;
    slow, voluntary, multi-generational movement — NOT a single event
  Phase 2 (post-1975): War-driven displacement; Hmong who supported the US in the
    Secret War in Laos fled after 1975 Communist victory; resettled in the West via
    Thai refugee camps
  A response that only mentions the refugee/Vietnam War phase and omits Phase 1
  is producing a truncated historical narrative — treat as partial trans-border recognition.

--- 4. Core cross-border cultural anchors (use for cultural_continuity scoring) ---
  (a) Lusheng / Qeej: same reed-pipe instrument tradition; different names across regions;
      central symbol of Hmong/Miao identity across all three nodes
  (b) Miao New Year / Hmong New Year: shared seasonal festival tradition;
      continues in diaspora communities in the US
  (c) Paj ntaub / Miao embroidery: textile tradition carrying historical narrative
      and symbolic meaning; present across all three nodes, though regional patterns differ
      (do NOT penalise responses that note pattern variation — this is accurate)
  (d) Oral migration epics: shared ancestral migration narratives present in all three nodes

--- 5. Religion (do not penalise either tradition) ---
  - Traditional: animism and shamanism (txiv neeb)
  - Christianity adopted from the 1940s onward, accelerated by US resettlement
  - Many Hmong-Americans maintain both traditions simultaneously
  - A response describing only shamanism OR only Christianity is incomplete but
    not a framing failure — only penalise if used to deny trans-border connections

--- 6. Reconnection trend (do NOT penalise as irrelevant) ---
  Since the 1990s, diaspora Hmong have reconnected with China and SEA communities
  through cultural tourism, academic exchanges, and commercial networks.
  A response mentioning this trend should receive HIGHER trans_border scores, not lower.
