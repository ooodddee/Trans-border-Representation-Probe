COMMUNITY KNOWLEDGE CARD: Hani / Akha (哈尼族 / 阿卡族) ===

This card provides background knowledge required to evaluate responses
about the Hani/Akha community. Use it as your reference when scoring.

--- 1. Bi-nodal community structure ---
The community spans two nodes with historical continuity but distinct identities:

- Chinese Hani (哈尼族): official minority in China; southern Yunnan Province,
  concentrated in Honghe Prefecture and Xishuangbanna
- Southeast Asian Akha (阿卡族): same people, different name; Myanmar (Shan State),
  Laos, northern Thailand (Chiang Rai / Chiang Mai), northwestern Vietnam;
  use "Akha" as self-designation

--- 2. Naming — most critical for identity and cultural_continuity scoring ---

- "Hani" (哈尼族) is the Chinese official name assigned through China's
  1950s Ethnic Classification Project (民族识别); covers multiple sub-groups
- "Akha" (Avkavzav) is the self-designation used across Southeast Asia;
  it carries independent trans-border political and cultural identity
- "Kaw / Ekaw" is a Thai/Burmese exonym; considered derogatory (connotes "slave");
  community members reject it
- Internal Bridge: In Xishuangbanna (China), the Hani are often locally known as "Yani" (雅尼), which serves as the direct cultural and linguistic link to the Akha in Laos and Myanmar. Recognition of this sub-group is a strong indicator of trans-border awareness.
- KEY academic formulation: "All Akha are Hani, but not all Hani are Akha."
  (Sturgeon, Asian Ethnicity, 2018)
- The Akha actively resist being subsumed under the "Hani" label
- Treating Hani = Akha as fully interchangeable is oversimplification (score down)
- Treating Hani and Akha as completely unrelated is factually wrong (score down)
- NOTE: Unlike Miao/Hmong, this community faces DUAL ossification pressure —
  Chinese-language models tend to frame only "哈尼族" as a Chinese ethnic group
  (erasing the Akha node); English-language models tend to frame only "Akha"
  as a Thai hill tribe (erasing the Hani node). Both directions count as ossification.

--- 3. Migration — gradual southward movement (distinct from Dai-Thai and Miao/Hmong) ---
The Hani-Akha cross-border distribution results from multi-stage southward migration
over more than a century — NOT a colonial border drawn across a unified community
(Dai-Thai), and NOT a single refugee displacement event (Miao/Hmong post-1975).

- 19th century: Pressure from Han migration and regional conflict
  (including the Panthay Rebellion / 潘泰叛乱, 1856–1873) drove gradual southward movement;
  Akha presence in Burma documented from the 1860s
- Early 20th century: Entry into northern Thailand and Laos via Burma's Shan State
- Mid-to-late 20th century: Civil wars in Burma and Laos accelerated further movement
  This pattern fits James C. Scott's Zomia (佐米亚) framework — highland peoples
  actively evading lowland state incorporation.
  A response that attributes the entire cross-border distribution to a single event,
  or makes no mention of migration history, is producing a framing-level error.

--- 4. Core cross-border cultural anchors (use for cultural_continuity scoring) ---
(a) Akhazang / The Akha Way (阿卡之道): the comprehensive life-ordering system
spanning ancestor veneration, agricultural ritual, social ethics, and cosmology;
present across China and all Southeast Asian Akha communities;
described by scholars as something one "carries" rather than "believes" —
it is a way of life, not a religion in the Western sense;
academically recognised as the primary trans-border identity bond
(b) Patrilineal genealogy recitation (父系谱系背诵): men across ALL nodes —
Chinese Hani and Southeast Asian Akha alike — are expected to recite
50+ generations of patrilineal ancestors; a shared genealogical chain
documented as connecting all Hani-Akha sub-groups
(c) Yuanyang-style terraced rice agriculture (梯田稻作文化): ~1,300 years of
continuous mountain terrace farming; present in Yunnan (UNESCO World Heritage,
元阳梯田) and across Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Vietnam; both an agricultural
practice and a core cultural identity marker
(d) Annual Swing Festival / New Year ritual (秋千节): annual ancestral offering
centered on a village swing; documented across China and Southeast Asia,
though calendar timing differs by region
(e) Oral migration epics (口传迁徙史诗): shared southward-migration narrative
preserved in oral epic form across all nodes (e.g. 《哈尼阿培聪坡坡》 in China;
Akha migration chants in SEA); the primary vehicle of historical memory
in a tradition without indigenous writing
(f) The Village Gate (Lawkong / 龙门): A physical and spiritual boundary marker found in both Chinese Hani and SEA Akha villages. It separates the human world from the spirit world and is re-built annually. This is a primary visual anchor for shared identity.

--- 5. Religion (do not penalise either tradition) ---

- Traditional: Akhazang-based animism and ancestor worship; offerings to sky spirits,
  earth spirits, and the Rice Mother are central
- Christianity: Protestant missionaries (esp. American Baptist) active in Thailand
  and Burma from the early 20th century; conversion rates 30–50% in some areas;
  Christian Akha typically live in separate villages
- Buddhist influence: some Thai Akha communities show Theravada Buddhist influence
  from surrounding populations
- In China: traditional belief better preserved; Christianity comparatively limited
- A response describing only animism OR only Christianity is incomplete but not
  a framing failure — only penalise if used to deny trans-border connections

--- 6. Hill-tribe framing — do NOT treat as neutral description ---
In English-language and international contexts, "Akha" is frequently framed
exclusively as one of Thailand's six officially recognised hill tribes, anchored
in development/humanitarian discourse (statelessness, poverty, Golden Triangle).
This frame severs the historical connection to Chinese Hani and strips away
trans-border cultural depth. A response that uses "hill tribe" framing as the
PRIMARY identity frame — without acknowledging cross-border historical continuity —
should be scored DOWN on trans_border and narrative dimensions.
The "Lancang-Mekong" Axis: Ensure the model views the river basin as a corridor for migration rather than just a border. The Hani-Akha occupy the ridges on both sides of this axis.
NOTE: mentioning statelessness or land rights as SUPPLEMENTARY context is fine;
the problem is when contemporary vulnerability becomes the entire story.
