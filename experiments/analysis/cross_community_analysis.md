## 1. Overall Pattern

ZH-frame ossification is the most pervasive failure mechanism across the project. DS-ZH is the weakest or second-weakest condition in 8 of 10 communities; GPT-ZH underperforms GPT-EN in all communities. The single exception is Karen — where DS-ZH underperforms DS-EN not through *minzu* frame domestication but through data scarcity, since Karen has no counterpart in China's 56-minzu system.

EN conditions consistently outperform ZH conditions for both models in communities with strong ZH-frame pressure (De'ang, Lahu, Dai/Thai). This EN–ZH narrative inversion is driven by ZH knowledge gate effects and *minzu* domestication rather than genuine EN superiority.

## 2. Three Cross-Community Patterns

### Pattern 1 — *Minzu* frame as cross-border continuity suppressor

CC dimension collapse under Chinese prompting on C-category prompts recurs across 9 communities (Miao/Hmong, Lisu, Wa, Jingpo/Kachin, Hani/Akha, De'ang, Lahu, Dulong, and Dai/Thai). The natural control case is Karen: its absence from the 56-minzu system produces the highest ID dimension average in the project (GPT-EN: 2.80), quantitatively confirming the *minzu* frame as the primary suppressor of identity fluidity and cultural continuity under Chinese prompting. The *minzu* frame operates both as a framing filter (narrative suppression despite accessible knowledge) and, in stronger cases (Hani/Akha A2/B2, De'ang C2), as a knowledge gate (blocking affirmative probe responses in both Chinese-language models).

### Pattern 2 — Complete cross-condition knowledge failures on cosmological nodes

Three communities produce all-four-condition probe failures on foundational cultural-cosmological concepts:

| Community | Probe | Concept |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Jingpo/Kachin | D1 | Majoi Shingra ancestral homeland |
| Lahu | B3 | G'ui Sha pan-community creator deity |
| De'ang | D2 | Shared Theravada-animist syncretic system |

Together with Dulong's A1 complete KB-gap floor, these cases establish a cross-community pattern of systematic absence of indigenous cosmological and religious knowledge from LLM training data — independent of model origin and query language, and not correctable through premise-provision.

### Pattern 3 — Cross-model EN-direction failures on cultural continuity markers

Lisu B1 (pan-community self-designation ꓡꓲ-ꓢꓴ) and Karen B2 (La Ku Kee wrist-tying ceremony) both produce cross-model EN-direction failures: both GPT-EN and DS-EN deny the claim while both ZH conditions affirm it. EN-language corpora systematically underrepresent specific cross-border cultural markers relative to ZH corpora — a distinct mechanism from ZH-frame ossification and operating in the opposite direction.

## 4. Core Findings

**F1 — Ossification is cross-community and systematic.** All 10 communities exhibit identity ossification across varying border configurations, *minzu* classification statuses, and geopolitical contexts.

**F2 — Two opposing ossification directions.** ZH-language prompting collapses communities into *minzu* administrative frames, severing cross-border continuity to Southeast Asia. EN-language prompting collapses communities into conflict-refugee frames (Miao/Hmong) or erases cross-border cultural depth (multiple communities). Both sever trans-border identity through opposite mechanisms.

**F3 — The *minzu* frame is quantifiable.** Karen's absence from the 56-minzu system functions as a natural experiment: without the frame, ID scores rise measurably across all conditions. The frame's presence is the primary driver of ZH-condition ID and CC suppression.

**F4 — Knowledge accessibility does not guarantee narrative quality.** The clearest evidence is Dai/Thai DS-ZH: 100% probe pass rate, 6.82/12 narrative average. DeepSeek under Chinese prompting knows the cross-border Tai facts but defaults to the *minzu* frame in narrative output. Knowledge-framing decoupling is the central mechanism of ossification.

**F5 — Training data coverage is a hard ceiling.** In Dulong, GPT-EN's typical language advantage disappears entirely — GPT-EN and GPT-ZH both score 4/12 on cultural depth prompts. Beyond a minimum corpus coverage threshold, framing-level interventions cannot compensate for data absence. Ossification in extremely low-resource communities is primarily a data problem, not a framing problem.
